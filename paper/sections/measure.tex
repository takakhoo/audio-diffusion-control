\section{What We Measure}

For each prompt and seed we render the same initial noise at every slider position and call the result a trajectory. Unless stated, every number uses 24 prompts held out from training, three seeds, and ten-second clips.

\textbf{Does a measured property follow the slider?} Each slider is paired, before any training, with a descriptor computed from the waveform with no learned model in the loop: spectral centroid, onset rate, beat-tracked tempo \citep{foscarin2024beatthis}, energy decay time, percussive energy share, low-band and stereo-side energy ratios, key clarity, major-minus-minor key fit, harmonic change rate. We report the mean Spearman correlation between position and descriptor over trajectories ($\rho$), the share of trajectories whose two ends are in the intended order, and the change in units of the descriptor's standard deviation across unsteered clips.

\textbf{Does a second model agree?} The projection of each clip on the slider's text direction is computed in CLAP and again in MuQ-MuLan, which shares no weights or training data with it.

\textbf{Does it still sound like music?} Every clip is scored by Audiobox Aesthetics \citep{tjandra2025aesthetics} (content enjoyment, CE, on a 1--10 scale) and by SongEval \citep{yao2025songeval} (musicality, 1--5), a head on MuQ features trained on human ratings of generated songs. Both are anchored by 2{,}000 excerpts of real recordings, so that a number means something. The \emph{usable span} of a slider is the set of positions, walking outward from zero, over which mean CE stays within 0.5 of the unsteered clips. Effect sizes are reported inside it, which stops a slider from being credited for change it only achieves by destroying the clip.

\textbf{How much of the piece survives?} CLAP similarity between each clip and the same trajectory at position zero.

\textbf{What else moves?} The slope of every descriptor against position, in standard deviations per unit, for every slider: a leakage matrix. A panel of 80 instrument, genre, mood, and character tags, scored in CLAP, puts the change into words.

\textbf{Coverage.} For a direction $d$ found in real recordings, the coverage of a generator is the standard deviation of its clips' projections on $d$ divided by that of the recordings. We report it over all prompts, and again after removing each prompt's mean from the generated clips and each genre's mean from the recordings, which is what changing only the seed can reach. A slider's reach along $d$ is reported in the same unit.
