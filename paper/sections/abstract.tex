\begin{abstract}
A slider is a low-rank update to a frozen generative model whose strength is set at inference time, so that one prompt and one seed can be moved along a single axis. Sliders have been ported from images to audio, where they are scored by text--audio similarity in a learned embedding. We ask three questions of a slider for music that this score does not answer: does a property measured from the waveform follow it, is the result still the same piece, and does it still sound like music. We train sliders on two open text-to-music models, Stable Audio Open 1.0 and ACE-Step 1.5, in two ways: from a prompt pair, and from two sets of the model's own clips with no text, sorted by a signal descriptor, by an aesthetics score, or along a principal direction of their embeddings. On \nPrompts{} held-out prompts, musical sliders on ACE-Step (mood, harmony, ensemble size, melody) move their descriptors monotonically while content enjoyment stays above the mean of \nReal{} real recordings. Switching a slider on only after the first \gateSteps{} of 50 sampling steps raises similarity to the unsteered piece from \gateKeepOff{} to \gateKeepOn{} and cuts the loss in enjoyment from \gateLossOff{} to \gateLossOn{} points. Two text sliders that pass the embedding test fail the waveform test: note density and percussion move in CLAP space while onset rate and percussive energy do not follow. Principal components of one concept's clips carry up to \pcaTop{}\% of their variance and read as musical contrasts once labelled with tags. Code, sliders, tables, and an audio demo are public.
\end{abstract}
