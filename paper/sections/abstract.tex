\begin{abstract}
A slider is a small low-rank update to a frozen music generator whose strength is chosen at sampling time, so that one prompt and one seed can be nudged along a single axis: a little happier, a little fuller, a little brighter. Sliders borrowed from image diffusion are usually judged by whether the output drifts toward the right words in a text--audio embedding. We ask what that leaves out. On Stable Audio Open and ACE-Step we test whether a property measured from the waveform follows the slider, whether two unrelated quality predictors still hear music, how much of the original piece survives, and what else moved. Two sliders pass the embedding test and fail the waveform test. A trained slider matches prompt-pair guidance at a third of the cost, and interpolating the prompt breaks the audio. We then let real music choose the axes: independent components of embeddings of \covReal{} recordings include arousal and valence, and the generator's own output spans only \covLo{}--\covHi{}\% of the real spread along them. Sliders trained along these axes, from the axis's own tags or with no text at all, follow them on held-out prompts (rank correlation up to 0.91) and carry a clip across the mean of real music while both quality predictors stay level.
\end{abstract}
