\begin{abstract}
A slider is a low-rank update to a frozen generative model whose strength is set at inference time, so one prompt and seed can be moved along a single axis. Audio sliders are scored by text--audio embedding similarity. We ask three questions that leaves open: does a property measured from the waveform follow the slider, is the result still the same piece, and does it still sound like music. We train sliders on Stable Audio Open 1.0 and ACE-Step 1.5 in two ways: from a prompt pair, and from two sets of the model's own clips with no text, sorted by a descriptor, an aesthetics score, or a principal direction of their embeddings. On \nPrompts{} held-out prompts, sliders for mood, harmony, ensemble size, and melody move their descriptors monotonically while content enjoyment stays above the mean of \nReal{} real recordings. Switching a slider on after the first \gateSteps{} of 50 sampling steps raises similarity to the unsteered piece from \gateKeepOff{} to \gateKeepOn{}. Sliders for note density and percussion on Stable Audio Open pass the embedding test and fail the waveform test. Principal components of one concept's clips carry up to \pcaTop{}\% of their variance and read as musical contrasts.
\end{abstract}
