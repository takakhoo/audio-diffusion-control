\begin{abstract}
A slider is a low-rank update to a frozen generative model whose strength is set at inference time, so one prompt and seed can be moved along a single axis. Sliders for music are scored by text--audio embedding similarity, on axes someone chose by writing a prompt pair. We measure what that leaves out, on Stable Audio Open and ACE-Step: whether a waveform property follows the slider, whether two unrelated quality predictors still rate the result as music, how much of the piece survives, and what else moves. A trained slider reproduces prompt-pair guidance with a third of the forward passes, activation steering is competitive, and prompt interpolation fails. Two sliders pass the embedding test and fail the waveform test. We then take axes from real music: independent components of embeddings of \covReal{} recordings include arousal and valence, and the model's output spans only \covLo{}--\covHi{}\% of the real spread along them. Sliders trained with no text along these axes follow them on held-out prompts (rank correlation up to \axRhoHi{}) and move a clip up to 1.19 real-music standard deviations with no loss in predicted quality. Principal axes of the generator's own activations describe production instead.
\end{abstract}
