\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Prompt-pair sliders.} Content enjoyment averages \fmaCE{} over \nReal{} real recordings, \aceCEzero{} for unsteered ACE-Step clips and \mainCEzero{} for Stable Audio Open. Table~\ref{tab:ace} and Figure~\ref{fig:ace} report eight ACE-Step sliders over nine positions. Where a descriptor was assigned in advance it rises with the slider, and MuQ-MuLan agrees with CLAP on the direction for all eight. Enjoyment stays above the real-music mean across the range for all but tension, whose usable span ends at $+1$. SongEval gives the same picture from different training data: unsteered clips score 2.77 in musicality against 2.78 for the real recordings, the usable span it implies is identical for six of the eight sliders and half a step wider for the other two, and the two predictors agree over clips with Spearman 0.52--0.67. Sliders trained on ten-second clips carry over to thirty seconds: brightness stays ordered on every trajectory ($\rho=1.00$), ensemble on 92\%, and unsteered enjoyment is 7.34.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Four ways to move the same attribute.} Table~\ref{tab:methods} runs the alternatives on the same prompts and seeds. The trained slider reproduces prompt-pair guidance, its own training target, with one forward pass per step instead of three: it is higher on brightness and harmony and within the confidence interval elsewhere. Activation steering is within 0.05 of the slider on four of six descriptors and keeps more of the piece (0.83--0.87 against 0.74--0.84), while the slider moves its descriptor further on brightness, ensemble, and harmony. Interpolating the text conditioning has the lowest correlation on every attribute and breaks the audio at the ends.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{The embedding says yes, the waveform says no.} On Stable Audio Open, sliders trained from the prompt pairs for note density and percussion move the output along their CLAP direction at every setting, and neither moves its descriptor: the rank correlation with onset rate or percussive share never exceeds \weakRho{}. Scored by embedding alone both would be reported as working. The descriptor has to be sound too: with a tempo estimate prone to octave errors the tempo slider scored $\rho=-0.01$, and with a beat tracker it scores $0.57$ and moves the mean tempo from 90 to 167 BPM.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Gating.} On the 50-step model a slider at $\pm1$ takes the piece with it. Switched on from step \gateSteps{}, the brightness slider keeps \gateRangeOn{} of its \gateRangeOff{} standard deviations of range, similarity to the unsteered clip rises from \gateKeepOff{} to \gateKeepOn{}, and the loss in enjoyment falls from \gateLossOff{} to \gateLossOn{} points (Table~\ref{tab:gating}).

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{What else moves, and cancelling it.} The mood and melody sliders both raise the spectral centroid by 0.8 standard deviations per unit. Adding the brightness slider at $-0.53$ and $-0.55$ times their position brings the leak to $-0.02$ and $+0.09$, raises similarity to the unsteered clip at $\pm1$ from 0.85 to 0.90 and from 0.87 to 0.92, and lowers the CLAP direction correlation from 0.92 to 0.69 and from 0.93 to 0.72. Part of what the embedding scores as happier or more melodic is brightness.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Axes of real music.} Fitted on two halves of the recordings and matched one to one, the leading eight axes agree with mean cosine 0.98 (MuQ-MuLan, principal), 0.94 (CLAP, principal), 0.90 (MuQ-MuLan, independent), and 0.83 (CLAP, independent). Of 1{,}024 sparse-autoencoder features on CLAP, only 9 are found by two seeds. Against a harder test, axes refitted on 47{,}942 further recordings from a different slice of the archive, the leading subspace carries over (the top 32 directions overlap 97\%) while single axes rotate inside it: the matched cosine of the MuQ-MuLan independent components falls to 0.61. The independent components of MuQ-MuLan read most like music: one runs from quiet, minor-key, and dreamy to aggressive, dry, and rhythmic, another from dark and distorted to playful and simple, the arousal and valence of the circumplex model \citep{russell1980circumplex}; others contrast jazz with electronic music, plucked and bowed strings with synthesizer and choir, and piano with organ and rock. Arousal, valence, and the jazz--electronic axis reappear in the second corpus with the same tags (cosine 0.65--0.76); the strings--synthesizer axis does not.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Coverage.} Along every one of these axes the model's output is narrower than real music (Figure~\ref{fig:coverage}). Over 648 prompts crossing genre, instrument, and mood, ACE-Step spans \covLo{}--\covHi{}\% of the real spread; seeds of one prompt span \covSeedLo{}--\covSeedHi{}\% of what recordings of one genre span. The average generated clip sits 1.35 standard deviations toward the quiet end of the arousal axis and 1.36 toward the playful end of the valence axis. On CLAP's first three principal axes ACE-Step covers 35--39\% and Stable Audio Open 48--52\%. The top eight principal directions of the generated corpus contain \covOverlap{}\% of the top eight of the real one.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Axes inside the generator.} Eight principal axes carry 90\% of the within-prompt variance of ACE-Step's cross-attention outputs and reproduce across disjoint prompt sets (0.82). Steering along the first two raises level and lowers spectral flatness ($\rho=0.77$ and $-0.64$) and moves tags from lo-fi toward complex and live. The other six leave at least 95\% similarity to the unsteered clip at twelve natural standard deviations and act differently from prompt to prompt (consistency 0.10--0.21). Unlike the image case \citep{haas2023directions}, the leading directions here describe production; arousal, valence, and instrument contrasts appeared only in embeddings of real music.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Training between sets of real recordings.} With $H$ and $L$ drawn from the real recordings themselves, the slider damaged the clip equally in both directions. A penalty that ties the mean of its predictions at $\pm1$ to the frozen model removed the damage and left almost no steering: tempo did not move and major-minor fit moved from 0.09 to 0.15. Real music was useful for finding axes and the model's own clips for training along them.
