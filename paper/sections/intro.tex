\section{Introduction}

A text-to-music model takes a prompt and returns a clip. A musician who likes the clip but wants it a little happier, or fuller, or with richer harmony, has to rewrite the prompt and accept a different piece. A slider offers something else: keep the prompt and the seed, and move the result along one axis by a chosen amount.

Sliders exist for image diffusion as low-rank adapters with a runtime scale \citep{gandikota2024sliders}, and the idea of discovering the axes themselves comes from SliderSpace \citep{gandikota2025sliderspace}. Both have reached audio models as baselines \citep{ezra2025freesliders,staniszewski2026tada}. In that work a control counts as working when the output moves toward the attribute text in a joint text--audio embedding and stays close to the original.

For music that leaves four things open. The embedding can move while the property a listener would name does not. A control can reach its target by wrecking the clip, and similarity to the original does not say whether what remains is music. Nothing in the score says what else moved. And the axes are chosen by whoever writes the prompt pair, with no evidence that they are the directions along which music varies or that the model can reach their ends.

This paper measures those things on two open backbones. Its contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item A protocol that scores a control on a waveform descriptor fixed in advance, in two unrelated embedding models, on two unrelated quality predictors anchored by real recordings, on similarity to the unsteered piece, and on what else moved. Effect is reported only over the \emph{usable span}, the positions at which quality has not dropped.
\item A comparison of four ways to move the same attribute under that protocol. A trained slider reproduces prompt-pair guidance with one forward pass per step instead of three, activation steering is competitive with both, and interpolating the text conditioning fails. Two sliders pass the embedding test and fail the waveform test.
\item Axes taken from real music. Independent components of embeddings of \covReal{} recordings include arousal and valence. Projected on these axes, ACE-Step's output over 648 prompts spans \covLo{}--\covHi{}\% of the spread of real recordings, and Stable Audio Open shows the same pattern.
\item Sliders along those axes, trained two ways: from a prompt pair built from the axis's own tags, and with no text from two sets of the model's clips sorted along the axis. Both follow the axis; the first moves further, the second keeps more of the piece and is more selective, and a graded form of it reaches $\pm2$. We also report what did not work: training between sets of real recordings, and principal axes of the generator's own activations, which turn out to describe production rather than musical content.
\end{itemize}
