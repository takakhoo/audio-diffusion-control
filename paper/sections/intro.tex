\section{Introduction}

A text-to-music model takes a prompt and returns a clip. A musician who likes the clip but wants it a little happier, or fuller, or with richer harmony, has to rewrite the prompt and accept a different piece. A slider offers something else: keep the prompt and the seed, and move the result along one axis by a chosen amount.

Sliders exist for image diffusion as low-rank adapters with a runtime scale \citep{gandikota2024sliders}, and the idea of discovering the axes themselves, with no names supplied, comes from SliderSpace \citep{gandikota2025sliderspace}. Both have reached audio models as baselines \citep{ezra2025freesliders,staniszewski2026tada}. In that work a slider counts as working when the output moves toward the attribute text in a joint text--audio embedding and stays perceptually close to the original.

For music that leaves three things unmeasured. The embedding can move without the audio changing in the way a listener would name: Section~\ref{sec:results} shows two sliders whose embedding score rises while the descriptor they are named after stays flat. A slider can reach its target by wrecking the clip, and similarity to the original does not say whether what remains is music. And nothing in the score says what else moved.

This paper is about measuring those things, and about a second way to train a slider that follows from the measurements. Its contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item A protocol that scores a slider on a waveform descriptor fixed in advance, on a reference-free quality predictor anchored by real recordings, on similarity to the unsteered piece, and on a tag panel that puts the change in words. It reports effect only over the \emph{usable span}, the positions at which quality has not yet dropped.
\item Evidence on two open backbones, a diffusion model and a few-step rectified-flow model, that prompt-pair sliders for musical attributes pass this protocol, and that two acoustic ones pass the embedding test and fail the waveform test.
\item A trainer that needs no text: one low-rank update trained with opposite signs on two sets of the model's own clips. It turns any per-clip measurement into a slider, including a quality score and discovered principal directions, and does not need a differentiable path through the decoder and the embedding model.
\item A measurement of timestep gating: leaving the earliest sampling steps unsteered keeps the piece and its quality at a known cost in range.
\end{itemize}
